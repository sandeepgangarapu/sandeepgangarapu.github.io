So why might AI invent a court case?

Percentages illustrative. Try real ones in Transformer Explainer.

  • Trained on huge amounts of text to guess what comes next.
  • It holds a ranked list of guesses. That's why you get a different answer each time.
  • Context shifts the odds: "8 a.m." makes "class" likely.
  • A real-sounding case name is a likely phrase. Whether the case exists isn't part of the prediction.

Which word matters? What does "it" mean?

If there's time: FT visual explainer

  • Version 1: "it" is the trophy. One word changes: now it's the suitcase.
  • You knew by looking back at "big" or "small."
  • That's attention: for every word, the model weighs which other words matter.
  • It's the core of the transformer, the T in GPT.

"So it's just autocomplete?"

"…and in the last chapter, the detective revealed that the killer was  "

To predict that word, you have to understand the whole book.

  • "Just autocomplete" undersells it.
  • To guess the killer you need the plot, the clues, who lied.
  • Getting very good at the next word forces a model to learn grammar, facts and reasoning.
  • It can still be wrong, and it doesn't know when.

From word predictor to agent

Step 1 · Pretraining

Read almost everything

Predict the next word across the internet, books and code.

Step 2 · Fine-tuning

Learn to be helpful

Train on good answers and people's ratings.

Step 3 · Tools in a loop

Act, look, repeat

Search, click, run code, check, until it thinks it's done.

It picks likely words → typical answers, equally sure when wrong

It loops toward the goal → pushes past obstacles, even deliberate ones

  • Step 1: read almost everything, learn to predict.
  • Step 2: learn to answer like an assistant.
  • Step 3: give it tools and a loop. That's the agent from Chapter 1.
  • Two consequences: it drifts to the typical answer and sounds sure either way, and it keeps going toward the goal.
  • Both are weak spots.

Chapter 4

Stay in charge

What if the goal isn't to defeat it, but to be exceptionally good at working with it?

  • In the movie, they won by understanding the technology well enough to use it.
  • Same here.

Tricks fade. Your context doesn't.

Fading

Prompt tricks

"Let's think step by step" mattered in 2022. Models do it on their own now.

Staying

Skills

Written instructions for how your team does a task. An SOP for your AI.

AI doesn't learn on the job. Whoever writes the business down becomes valuable.

  • Prompt tricks fade with every release.
  • What doesn't fade: what the model can't know. Your customers, your standards, what your boss cares about.
  • Write it down once; the AI uses it every time. That's a skill.
  • That's a business person's job, not an engineer's.

The bigger the task AI takes on, the more each of these matters.

  • Knowing what good looks like: picking one of twenty.
  • Knowing your context: your business, written down.
  • Checking the work: is it true, did it do what it says.
  • Deciding what it can do: the grid.
  • Owning the result: your name is on it.
  • You're no longer checking a sentence. You're checking a day of work.
