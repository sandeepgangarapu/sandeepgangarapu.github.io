శ్రీ వేంకటేశ్వర సుప్రభాతమ్Śrī Veṅkaṭeśvara Suprabhātam

The dawn hymn sung every morning at Tirumala to wake Lord Veṅkaṭeśvara. It has 70 verses in four parts. Work through it one verse at a time: read the meaning, learn the lines, then test yourself.

Script
Practice

How to learn it

  1. Listen first. The speaker button next to each line plays that line from M.S. Subbulakshmi's recording, the version most people know. Most verses in Part 1 share one meter and one tune, so once verse 3 is in your ear, the rest follow. Find the recording on YouTube.
  2. Read the meaning before you memorize. Pictures like the parrots, the sages and the seven hills stick better than syllables.
  3. Learn two lines at a time. Read them, switch to Hints, then to Recall and say each line before you tap it.
  4. Gold lines are refrains that repeat across many verses. You will know them after a few verses.
  5. Turn on Long syllables while learning to pronounce. Hold the bold syllables about twice as long as the others.

About the hymn

Prativādi Bhayaṅkaram Aṇṇan, a disciple of the Śrīvaiṣṇava teacher Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ, wrote it in the 15th century. It is sung at the sanctum door at Tirumala before dawn every day.

It opens with a verse from Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa: the sage Viśvāmitra waking the young Rāma. The four parts follow the arc of a morning: waking the Lord, praising him, surrendering at his feet and wishing him well.

Audio: M.S. Subbulakshmi, Sri Venkatesa Suprabhatam (HMV, 1963).
