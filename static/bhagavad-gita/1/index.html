The Gītā opens on the battlefield of Kurukṣetra. Sañjaya tells the blind king Dhṛtarāṣṭra what he sees: the two armies, the conches, and Arjuna, who looks across at his own family and puts down his bow. It has 47 verses in four scenes. Work through it one verse at a time: read the meaning, learn the lines, then test yourself.

How to learn it

  1. Listen first. The speaker button next to each line plays that line from Swami Brahmananda’s chanting, the recitation used by the Gita Supersite at IIT Kanpur. Every verse in this chapter is in the same anuṣṭubh meter and is chanted to the same tune, so once the first few verses are in your ear, the rest follow.
  2. Know who is speaking. Sañjaya narrates to the blind king, Duryodhana speaks to his teacher Droṇa in verses 3 to 11, and Arjuna speaks to Kṛṣṇa from verse 28 on. A gold label marks the line where a new speaker begins. Chant it as part of that line: arjuna uvāca means “Arjuna said.”
  3. Read the meaning before you memorize. The story will carry you through the long lists of warriors and conches.
  4. Learn one line at a time. Read it, switch to Hints, then to Recall and say each line before you tap it.
  5. Turn on Long syllables while learning to pronounce. Hold the bold syllables about twice as long as the others.

About the chapter

The Bhagavad Gītā is a conversation between Kṛṣṇa and the warrior Arjuna, set in the Mahābhārata just before the great war at Kurukṣetra. The blind king Dhṛtarāṣṭra hears it from his minister Sañjaya, to whom the sage Vyāsa has given the sight to watch the battle from afar.

This first chapter sets the scene. The armies line up, the conches sound, and Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to drive his chariot between them. Seeing his grandfather, teachers and cousins on the other side, he is overcome with grief and sits down, his bow set aside. Kṛṣṇa’s teaching begins in Chapter 2. Every verse here is in the anuṣṭubh meter: two lines of sixteen syllables each.

Closing words, chanted after verse 47

ఓం తత్సదితి శ్రీమద్భగవద్గీతాసు ఉపనిషత్సు బ్రహ్మవిద్యాయాం యోగశాస్త్రే శ్రీకృష్ణార్జునసంవాదే అర్జునవిషాదయోగో నామ ప్రథమోఽధ్యాయః
oṁ tatsaditi śrīmadbhagavadgītāsu upaniṣatsu brahmavidyāyāṁ yogaśāstre śrīkṛṣṇārjunasaṁvāde arjunaviṣādayogo nāma prathamo'dhyāyaḥ

Oṁ tat sat. So ends the first chapter, called the Yoga of Arjuna’s Despair, in the Upaniṣad of the glorious Bhagavad Gītā: the knowledge of Brahman, the scripture of yoga, the dialogue between Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna.
